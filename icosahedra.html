An Alternative Census of Icosahedral Stellations

The formal enumeration of the stellations of the regular icosahedron has traditionally been governed by the constraints set forth in the 1938 text The Fifty-Nine Icosahedra. The classic list of 59 models remains the standard paradigm today, but it relies on restrictive rules about face-continuity and edge-to-edge linkages across the stellation diagram lines.

This project presents a complete, unconstrained enumeration of the icosahedral cell decomposition space. By discarding arbitrary edge-continuity rules and evaluating the system purely from first principles of solid mechanics and visual boundary mapping, the baseline combinatorial search space collapses not to 59, but to exactly 205 unique, physically distinct polyhedral configurations.

The Foundational Axioms

This census evaluates the 4096 binary permutations of the 12 independent, atomic cell types of the icosahedron using only two structural rules:

Axiom 1: Full Icosahedral Symmetry (The Atomic Set Rule)
Cells belonging to the same symmetry type — treating the chiral enantiomorphs of the f1 shell as distinct structural entities — must be included or excluded as a complete set.
Axiom 2: Topological Solid-Equivalence (The Reachability Filter)
If two distinct binary configurations of cells produce identical external boundaries, they are merged into a single equivalent entry. Any internal cavity or hollow space that is sealed off from the outside is changed to solid matter.
